## ⚡ THE BUSINESS PROBLEM

Discounts can increase customer demand.

But higher discounts can also reduce profit margins.

The project investigates:

```text
DISCOUNT
    +
PRODUCT PERFORMANCE
    +
CUSTOMER BEHAVIOR
    +
REGIONAL PERFORMANCE
        ↓
PROFITABILITY
```

The objective was to identify:

* How discount levels affect profitability
* Where profit losses occur
* Which products contribute to losses
* Which customers may generate profit leakage
* Which geographic areas require attention
* Where discount strategy should be reviewed

# 🔥 THE DISCOUNT CLIFF

The analysis identified a clear pattern between discount levels and profitability.

| DISCOUNT RANGE | PROFIT PERFORMANCE |
|---|---|
| 🟢 **0%** | $320,988 profit |
| 🟢 **0–10%** | $9,029 profit |
| 🟢 **10–20%** | $91,756 profit |
| 🔴 **20–30%** | -$10,369 loss |
| 🔴 **30–40%** | -$25,448 loss |
| 🔴 **40–50%** | -$22,999 loss |
| 🚨 **50%+** | -$76,559 loss |

<br>

<div align="center">

# 🚨 KEY FINDING

## DISCOUNTS ABOVE 20% CONSISTENTLY GENERATED LOSSES.

</div>

# 💡 BUSINESS RECOMMENDATIONS

## 01 — CONTROL HIGH DISCOUNTS

The analysis indicates that discounts above 20% consistently resulted in negative profitability.

**Recommendation:** Establish discount thresholds and review exceptions individually.

---

## 02 — REVIEW HIGH-LOSS CUSTOMERS

High sales do not automatically mean high profitability. Customer-level analysis should be used to identify accounts where discounting contributes to losses.

---

## 03 — INVESTIGATE HIGH-LOSS STATES

Possible investigation areas:

* Discount approval
* Product mix
* Pricing
* Customer behavior
* Profit margins

---

## 04 — APPLY PRODUCT-SPECIFIC DISCOUNT RULES

Different products have different profit margins. A single discount policy may expose low-margin products to significant losses.
